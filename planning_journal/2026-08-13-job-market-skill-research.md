# 2026-08-13 — Job market research to ground the skill gap matrix

**Context:** Month 1 of the roadmap calls for job specs analyzed + skill gap map done, before committing to the roadmap's assumed skill tiers. Wanted evidence instead of assumption.

**Decision:** Researched 9 real, current AI Engineer postings (remote-EU/Zurich-eligible, targeting €100k+/$150k+) and used the findings plus Bruno's self-rated current levels to fill `07_career_prep/SKILL_GAP_MATRIX.md`.

**Findings that changed the plan:**
- Full-stack product ability (React/Node) showed up as a real differentiator in postings (3/9) — the original roadmap didn't call this out at all, even though it's Bruno's existing core strength. Decision: lean into it explicitly in portfolio positioning rather than treating it as separate from "AI engineering."
- Fine-tuning/LoRA/QLoRA/RLHF only appeared in one outlier posting (PhD, 7+ years, $215k+ research role) — the roadmap's Tier 2 placement looks overstated for the €100-150k band actually being targeted. Downgraded target level to light exposure (2/5), not deep competency.
- Evals/observability tooling was flagged as what separates senior/€100k+ postings from generalist ones — reinforces keeping this mid-late in the roadmap but signals it's higher-leverage than the roadmap text suggests.
- RAG is the single biggest gap between "most in-demand" and "current skill level" (0/5) — good candidate for where to focus first once foundations are done.

**Why:** Roadmaps written in advance are best guesses. Real job postings are ground truth for what's actually being screened for right now.

**Reconsider if:** the market shifts noticeably (e.g. MCP becomes a standard JD requirement rather than 1/9), or once actual applications start and interview feedback gives a second, higher-fidelity signal than job postings alone.
